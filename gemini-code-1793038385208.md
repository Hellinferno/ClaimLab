# Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon Participant Guide

Welcome to the *Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon* (the “Hackathon”)! We’re thrilled to have you on board.

This guide is your all-in-one resource for the event, including schedule, rules, technical resources, problem statements, judging information, and more. Please read this carefully; most answers can be found here.

## 1. Your Goal: Hackathon Problem Statements

Your project must build towards one of the following problem statements:

* **Build for Your World** – Bring Meta's models into your domain. Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space, and make something possible that wasn't before.
* **Push the Frontier** – Use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials, and beyond. Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a physical system. Show us what becomes possible when these models meet the real world.

Your project must incorporate Meta's models. Use the [Muse Spark series](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-spark), [Muse Wonder](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-wonder), [Muse Image](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-image), [Muse Voice Transcribe](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-voice-transcribe), and [SAM 3.1](https://dev.meta.ai/models/sam-3-1) through [Meta Model API](https://dev.meta.ai/products/meta-model-api); run Meta open weights models [Muse Glimmer](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-glimmer) and [Muse Spark Open](https://dev.meta.ai/models/muse-spark-open); and code faster with the [Muse Code](https://dev.meta.ai/lp/muse-code) coding agent.

## 4. Hackathon Rules

* **Open source:** Repositories *must be public*.
* **Team size:** A *maximum of four* participants per team. Solo participants are allowed.
* **Demo requirements:** Your demo *must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality that you built during the Hackathon*. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the event. Failure to clearly identify original contributions will result in immediate disqualification at the judges' sole discretion.
* **New work only:** What you present at this Hackathon must have been built during the event. This should be clear in your submissions. Startups may build on existing products or codebases, but newly developed work must be clearly delineated.
* **Banned projects:** Projects will be disqualified if they violate legal, ethical, or platform policies, or use code, data, or assets without proper rights.

### Sample Anti-Projects
The following list contains project types that are generic or violate platform policies. Avoid building:
* Any project using AI to generate and provide medical advice (e.g., AI Mental Health Advisor, skin blemish detector).
* Basic RAG Applications.
* Simple Image Analyzers.
* Simple “AI for Education” Chatbots.
* AI Job Application Screeners.
* AI Nutrition Coaches.
* Personality Analyzers.
* Sports analyzers or coaches.
* Anything political or involving war.

## 7. Submission Process

Solo participants, teams, and startups must [submit here](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon/hackathon/submit) by **November 4 at 9:00 PM EST**.
* Teams and startups submit one project collaboratively, not individual entries per member. Late submissions are not accepted.
* Each team must appoint one individual (the “Representative”) to act on behalf of the group.
* Ensure your repository is publicly accessible, your video link works, and all team contributors are listed on the submission page: [Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon – Cerebral Valley](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon/hackathon/submit).
* **Demo Video Requirement:** Submissions require a 3-minute demo video highlighting features, code, and functionality built during the Hackathon. This video serves as a primary artifact for evaluation alongside your code and live product.

## 8. Judging Process

Judging occurs across three distinct stages:

* **Stage One:** Pass/Fail evaluation confirming baseline viability—ensuring the project fits the theme and properly utilizes the Meta Model API and/or Meta open-weight models built during the hackathon.
* **Stage Two:** Submissions passing Stage One are scored from 0–10 based on the official rubric.
* **Stage Three:** The top 5 scoring projects per track advance to the final round, where judges watch video demos live and rank projects using the same rubric.

### Judging Rubric

| Category (Weight) | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Impact (30%)** | What's the real-world potential? Who benefits, and how much does it matter? Could this become something people use? Does it fit into one of the designated problem statements? |
| **Use of Meta AI Tooling (25%)** | How creatively did this team use Meta's AI tooling? Did they go beyond basic integration and surface unexpected capabilities? |
| **Technical Depth & Execution (20%)** | Is the engineering sound and thoughtfully refined? Does the implementation reflect real craftsmanship? |
| **Demo (25%)** | Is this a working, impressive demo? Does the product hold up live? Is the demo video compelling to watch? |
